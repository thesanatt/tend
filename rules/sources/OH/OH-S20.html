Overview

Safe at Home is part of Ohio’s comprehensive legal protections for crime victims and survivors. The program helps protect individuals who have experienced domestic violence, sexual assault, human trafficking, rape, sexual battery, or stalking by keeping their personal information confidential. Through Safe at Home, participants’ addresses, phone numbers, and other identifying details are hidden from public records, reducing the risk of being located and harmed again.

Who Safe at Home Protects

Under Ohio’s Safe at Home laws, the program provides confidentiality to survivors of:

  • Domestic violence

  • Human trafficking

  • Rape or sexual battery

  • Menacing by stalking

If you or someone in your household is a survivor of one of these crimes and fears for your safety, you may be eligible to participate in Safe at Home. All participant information is given a special legal protection status under R.C. 111.41 –111.47.

It is a criminal offense for any state or local government employee or official to unlawfully disclose confidential information belonging to a participant.

How to Apply for Safe at Home

Participants must apply through a certified Application Assistant. These assistants:

  • Work for organizations that serve survivors of domestic or sexual violence, human trafficking, or stalking.

  • Receive specialized training and are certified by the Ohio Secretary of State’s office.

  • Help applicants complete and submit all required forms safely and confidentially.

Once enrolled, participants receive a substitute address they can legally use for voter registration, government correspondence, and other official records.

Program Funding

Ohio’s court system partially funds the Safe at Home program. 
When individuals are convicted of certain crimes—such as domestic violence, rape, sexual battery, stalking, or human trafficking—the court may impose fines that help support the program. This ensures that the initiative remains sustainable and continues to protect survivors across the state.

Are You in Crisis?

If you are in danger or need immediate help, contact the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline by calling 988.

Trained counselors are available 24/7 to provide free and confidential support.

If you are a victim of domestic violence and are in danger or need immediate help contact the Ohio Domestic Violence Network or National Domestic Violence Hotline.
