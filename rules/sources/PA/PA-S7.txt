Eligibility for
Compensation
Survivors of sexual assault may
be eligible for one of two types of
claims.
1. Sexual Assault Counseling
Claim: This claim does not require
the crime to be reported to
authorities. If the crime was not
reported, the Program will
consider only counseling
expenses for reimbursement.
2. Standard Claim: If the assault
was reported to the appropriate
authorities—such as through a
PFA, SVPO, PFI, or the completion
of a forensic rape exam, —
survivors can submit a standard
claim. This allows for
compensation covering a range
of expenses, including lost
wages, relocation costs,
transportation, medications, and
other related expenses.

Compensation
Guidelines

More Flexibility in
Reporting/Filing

VCAP is a “payer of last resort”
meaning the program covers
costs not paid by other sources,
such as insurance. In certain
cases, victims may not be
required to use an offender’s
insurance to avoid further harm.

Thanks to Act 77 of 2022, victims
now have five years to file a
compensation claim, up from two
years, giving victims more time to
apply for compensation.
Reporting requirements have also
been changed to “promptly”
instead of the previous 72-hour
deadline, offering more flexibility

Confidentiality
Information collected during
claims is confidential and
privileged. Information cannot be
disclosed or used in legal
proceedings without written
consent from the victim,
intervenor, or their claimant if
deceased.

Online Claim
Submission
VCAP’s paperless process allows
victims, victim service programs
and allied professionals—
including therapists, hospitals,
and law enforcement agencies—
to upload documents through
the Dependable Access for
Victimization Expenses (DAVE)
system. Utilization of DAVE
speeds up processing, offers
real-time updates, and
enhances transparency.
