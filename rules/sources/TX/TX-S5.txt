A clear, complete application can be reviewed faster and reduces delays. Many people are unsure of what to include in their application or think they need to have everything ready before they start. You do not. This page explains what to include now and what can be sent later, so you can move forward with confidence. Who Can Apply You can apply if you are a victim of a crime or a claimant applying on behalf of a victim. If the victim is a child or has passed away, a parent, legal guardian, spouse, or another authorized person may apply. Before starting, please review Find Out if You Qualify and What Costs Can Be Covered. Before You Start Your Application Before you start your application, it helps to gather the information you already have. The most helpful applications tell us what happened, who reported the crime, include current contact information, and list any insurance or other resources that may help with costs. You do not need every document to begin. Even if you do not have bills yet, you can still apply now. Step 1. Create or Sign In to Your CVC Portal Account The CVC Portal is the fastest way to apply. Through the portal, you can submit your application online, upload billing documents, and check status updates. Note Each crime must have its own application. If you experienced more than one crime, you will need to submit a separate application for each crime date and report . Sign In to the CVC Portal If you would like instructions on how to use the portal, you can visit the CVC Portal Guide . If you cannot apply online, you may download a paper application here: English PDF | Español PDF or request one by calling (800) 983-9933. Step 2. Tell Us About the Crime We ask for information about the crime so we can request the official police report. The report confirms the crime, shows that it was reported to law enforcement, and provides the details we need to review your application. Without it, we cannot determine eligibility or process any payments. To request the report, we rely on the information you provide, which is the law enforcement agency name and the offense report number. You can apply without the offense report number, but this may delay your application if it takes longer to locate the correct report. If you do not know the offense report number, you can try: Calling the law enforcement agency that took the report Checking any paperwork or cards the responding officer gave you Getting help from a victim advocate Also, please write the full name of the law enforcement agency. Do not use abbreviations. For example, “SAPD” could refer to San Angelo Police Department or San Antonio Police Department. Providing the exact agency name and case number helps us request the report as quickly as possible. Step 3. Help Us Understand Your Needs During the application, we will ask what types of costs you need help with. Examples include medical care, counseling, lost wages, funeral expenses, and rent or relocation related to safety needs. Be as specific as you can because details help us review your claim faster. If something does not apply, write N/A or select No . Leaving items blank can delay your application while we contact you for follow-up information. Step 4. Submit Any Documents You Have If you have bills, invoices, letters, or insurance information, upload them in the portal. If you do not have a needed document yet, you can submit them later using the portal or the CVC Documentation Upload webform. Options That May Speed Up Your Application Review Some applications may be reviewed faster in certain situations. Presumptive Eligibility (advocate-assisted) : If you are working with a trained victim advocate, they may be able to submit your application through Presumptive Eligibility. In this process, the advocate gathers required documents and submits them directly for review. Learn more about Presumptive Eligibility Processing. Priority Processing (may be requested by anyone) : If you are experiencing an urgent financial need related to the crime, you may request Priority Processing. You will need to provide information that shows the urgent situation. We will review your request and let you know if your application qualifies. Learn more about Priority Processing. Get Help with Your Application (Optional) You can apply on your own, but many people find it helpful to work with a trained victim advocate. An advocate can: Explain your options Help gather documents Guide you through each step of the application Answer questions along the way You can contact: • Your local victim advocate • A family violence center • A rape crisis center • A Crime Victim Liaison at your police department What happens after you apply Here's what you can expect after submitting your application: Initial review Intake staff check that your application is complete Intake staff request the crime report from law enforcement (for CVC application) Intake staff lets you know if we need any additional information Eligibility review Once we have all the needed information, your claim goes to the eligibility team The eligibility team carefully reviews everything based on program requirements Processing time varies for each case, but we work as quickly as possible Decision and next steps The program sends the decision to you by mail If approved, if you’ll be assigned a case manager and we'll explain what costs can be covered If denied, we'll explain why and how you can appeal within 30 days. Learn more at the Appeals Process page. Note: Keep your mailing address up to date with our office The sooner you respond to requests for more information, the sooner we can process your payment Save all crime-related bills and receipts Contact us if you have any questions during the process You can check your claim status anytime by going to the Crime Victims’ Compensation Portal or by calling our hotline. Questions? If you have any questions or need help, don't hesitate to reach out. We're here to support you during this challenging time.
