Crime Victim Compensation - Frequently Asked Questions
What Benefits Are Available?




Maximum award up to $15,000 available during the claim period.
Crime related expenses can be considered up to 2 years from the crime date.
Mental health counseling expenses can be considered up to 3 years from the crime date.

What Types of Expenses Can Be Compensated?
Losses directly related to the compensable crime are eligible for reimbursement/payment directly
to the provider during the 2 year period of time a claim is open and include:











Medical/dental expenses
Mental health expenses ($85 per hour, associated victim max $4,500, 3 years for mental
health)
Lost wages (Federal Minimum Wage)
Loss of support (Federal Minimum Wage)
Funeral expenses (max $7000)
Crime scene clean-up (max $2500)
Items taken as evidence (max $500)
Certain out of pocket expenses (prescriptions)
Eye glasses replacement (max $300)
Mileage (to and from doctor’s appointment, etc.)

Catastrophic award up to an additional $10,000.00
 Catastrophic award may be made only for eligible losses and expenses
occurring within twenty-four (24) months after the date of the injury.
 Special renovations to home or car to accommodate disability resulting from
the victimization
 Lost Wages (after paid leave is exhausted)
 Special home health care, adaptive or medical equipment required as a result
of the victimization
o Prosthesis
o Wheel chairs or other adaptive devices
o Assisted technology items
 Cost associated with a Life Flight
Who Can File A Claim?





A victim of a crime who has suffered injury as a result of:
o a criminal act
o trying to stop a person committing a crime
o trying to help a law enforcement officer
o trying to help a victim of a crime
Families and dependents of deceased victims
Persons who are authorized to act on behalf of victims

What Are The Eligibility Requirements?










The crime must have occurred in Wyoming on or after May 23, 1985
The claim must be filed within one (1) year of the date of the crime
The victim or claimant must report the crime to law enforcement within a reasonable time
after the crime
The victim must have sustained an actual physical or mental harm as the result of a
criminal act of another
The victim or claimant must fully cooperate in the investigation and prosecution of the
crime
Federal crime victims are eligible to apply for compensation whether or not the crime
falls under tribal, state, or federal jurisdiction
No portion of the compensation shall benefit the offender in any way
A victim whose own misconduct either caused or contributed to the criminal act could be
reduced or denied compensation

Who Is NOT Eligible?







The offender and/or accomplice
A victim under indictment or convicted of a felony after applying for compensation
An individual who is a victim of a criminal attack while confined in a prison or other
correctional facility at the time of the crime
A victim whose expenses are paid entirely by other sources
Victims of monetary or property loss
Victims seeking compensation only for pain and suffering

What If There Is Insurance Or Another Source to Cover Part of the Losses?
Compensation is a secondary source which pays for losses that are not covered by a collateral
source (aka: the payer of last resort). Insurance benefits or any other collateral source must be
used first. If the collateral source does not pay the entire amount, then you may be eligible to be
compensated for what was not covered. The following are considered “collateral sources” that
must pay before compensation will reimburse a claimant:








Health Insurance
Automobile Insurance
Sick or Vacation Leave
Paid Time Off
Disability Insurance
Worker's Compensation
Social Security

Does There Have To Be An Arrest Or Conviction Before Compensation Will Be Paid


The only requirement to be eligible to receive compensation is that the crime MUST be
reported to law enforcement. No arrest, prosecution, nor conviction is necessary to be
eligible.

What If Restitution Is Recovered From The Offender?


In the event that the court orders the offender to make restitution, that portion of the
restitution covering expenses paid by the Division of Victim Services must be reimbursed
back to the compensation fund. If you recover money through a civil suit against the

offender, you will be required to pay the Division of Victim Services for expenses that
were paid on your behalf.
How Is The Claim Processed?






Upon receipt of the application, an investigation is conducted to verify all the
information. Law enforcement, service providers, employers, etc. are contacted to
substantiate the compensation application.
The Division staff reviews the claim and decides if it qualifies for compensation and
determines the amount(s) and category(s) the victim may be eligible for.
The Division will notify you of the amount of the award eligible for reimbursement. If
the claim is denied or payment reduced, the reason will be provided in writing.
If the claim is reduced or denied, the victim has the right to request an appeal within 30
days upon receipt of the reduction or denial.

How Do I Apply?
You must file an application within 1 year of the date of the crime.
Applications are available on this website. The Division of Victim Services strongly
recommends working with a local service provider to apply for compensation. Applications are
also available in your community at the following locations:





law enforcement agencies
domestic violence/ sexual assault agencies
prosecuting attorney's offices
victim assistance agencies
