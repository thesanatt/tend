Address Confidentiality Program

 
 

Connecticut Safe at Home: A Confidential Address Program for Domestic Violence, Sexual Assault, and Stalking Survivors

The Address Confidentiality Program (ACP), which became effective January 1, 2004, provides services to victims of crime. Program participants are residents of the State of Connecticut who have recently relocated and whose new location is unknown to the abuser and undocumented in government records. The goal of the ACP is to help victims keep their new residential addresses confidential.

ACP Information Brochure (click image to download)

  

Address Confidentiality Program (ACP) offers two services (both help keep the victim’s new location confidential):

  • ACP Substitute Mailing Address
    Each program participant is assigned a substitute mailing address. This substitute address has no relation to the participant’s actual location. The Office of the Connecticut Secretary of the State serves as each program participant’s legal agent for cost-free mail services (process, receipt, and forwarding of first-class mail). The ACP address is a post office box, and a "fictitious" street address located in Hartford, Connecticut.
     
    The Office of the Connecticut Secretary of the State issues certification cards to each program participant. The certification card includes the program participant’s name, signature, a certification code, the program address, and the certification expiration date. When a program participant presents their certification card, all public agencies (state and municipal) must accept the ACP substitute address as though it is a person’s actual residential address.
    The program then forwards first-class mail to each participant’s actual residential address.

    Please Note: All ACP participants use the same address. A program participant’s mail must include the corresponding certification code, and then the program forwards first-class mail to each participant's actual residential address.
     
    Program participants choose when to use the substitute address and may, if they wish, reveal their actual address to a government employee. When an ACP participant chooses to reveal their actual address, the public agency is not legally obligated to keep that information confidential. In unusual situations, an agency may petition the Office of the Connecticut Secretary of the State for an exemption to the ACP laws. If the Office of the Connecticut Secretary of the State grants the agency an ACP exemption, program participants involved with that agency may have to reveal their actual location. Prior to granting an exemption, the Office of the Connecticut Secretary of the State shall notify the program participant of the exemption, including the name of the agency and the reason(s) for the exemption. The Secretary of the State may not make any records in a program participant’s file, other than the program address, available for inspection or copying except: (1) when a written request is made by the head of a law enforcement agency or the State Elections Enforcement Commission; (2) when directed by court order to release the information; or (3) when the program participant’s certification has been cancelled. The Secretary of the State will respond to written requests to verify if a person is or is not a current program participant.
  • ACP Protected Records

    The ACP program provides three types of protection* of the victim’s actual residential address: 

  1. Marriage Certificates: The program participant may present their certification card and request that their marriage records be kept confidential.

  2. Voter Registration: The ACP participant’s actual address of residence (street, house number, and town) will be suppressed from the voter registration list upon presentation of the certification card to the town registrar of voters. Their street and house number will not be listed on a voter registry list; or that an agency uses their program address in lieu of their confidential address.

  3. State and Town Records: The ACP participant may use the "fictitious" address provided by our office in lieu of their actual physical address on any state or municipal form.

*Please Note: Marriage records and voter addresses will be kept confidential and will not be made available for inspection or copying except when: (1) requested by a law enforcement agency; (2) directed by court order; (3) or notified by the Secretary of the State that the program participant’s certification has been cancelled. Agencies will accept and use the program address as the program participant’s residential, work or school address, in lieu of the participant’s confidential address, unless the agency receives an exemption from the Office of the Connecticut Secretary of the State.

Applying for ACP Participation

The Address Confidentiality Program provides substitute address services to residents of Connecticut. Eligibility for residents is offered to the victim of the following crimes:
  • Family violence
  • Injury or risk of injury to a child
  • Kidnapping
  • Sexual assault
  • Stalking
  • Trafficking in persons
  • Child abuse or neglect, where such abuse or neglect was substantiated by the Department of Children and Families and was the basis for the issuance of a restraining order under section 46b-15 or civil protection order under section 46b-16a

All adults and children who meet the above criteria can participate in the program. Participants complete applications through a community-based domestic violence or sexual assault crisis program. These programs are located throughout the state. The application process involves meeting with an application assistant and receiving orientation information about the program. Application Assistants send the completed applications to the ACP office. The ACP office reviews the applications and then assigns the ACP substitute address, certification code, and creates an ACP Certification Card.
