Innocence and Contributory Conduct

For certain types of claims, your behavior is examined to determine if it caused or contributed to the
crime and/or injury. That said, contributory conduct is never used in screening claims involving
domestic violence, sexual assault, or human trafficking.
The Commission would never and has never treated a victim of a crime such as human trafficking as
an accomplice.
The cases in which a claim is denied because of contributory conduct or a lack of innocence have clear
criminal activity. Find below some recent examples of such conduct that resulted in dismissal of the
claim.
•

Fight between gang members.

•

Drug deal turned violent; further, applicant lied on application about being employed and refused
to cooperate with law enforcement.

•

Claimant fired first and was shot in response.

•

Victim was killed during a home invasion they perpetrated while armed with a gun; homeowner
took gun from the victim and shot them.

•

Victim was an active drug dealer and crime occurred during drug deal that turned violent. Such
situations have occurred a few times.

•

Victim was breaking down the door of girlfriend’s ex-boyfriend and homeowner shot victim.
