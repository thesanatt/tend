I.

Introduction
Since 2005, the New York State Office of Victim Services (OVS) has reimbursed hospitals, accredited
sexual assault examiner programs, and licensed healthcare providers (providers) for costs associated
with performing forensic rape exams (FREs), also known as sexual assault medical forensic exams.
A sexual assault medical forensic exam is an “examination provided to a victim of sexual assault by
medical personnel to gather evidence of a sexual assault in a manner suitable for use in a court of
law.”1 The examination includes, as appropriate, gathering information from the patient for the medical
forensic history; an examination; coordinating treatment of injuries; documentation of biological and
physical findings; medications and pharmaceuticals that are dispensed as part of the examination; and
information, treatment, and referrals for sexually transmitted infections (STIs), pregnancy, suicidal
ideation, alcohol and substance abuse, and other medical concerns; the examination may also involve
collection of evidence from the patient.2, 3, 4
OVS can directly reimburse providers for costs associated with sexual assault medical forensic exams
through FRE Direct Reimbursement Program claims (“FRE claims”). FRE claims are distinct from
general victim compensation claims that a victim or other eligible claimant submits to OVS for crimerelated reimbursement requests. FRE claims have statutorily defined reimbursement caps based on the
services provided to the victim.
Prior to June 2020, reimbursement was a flat rate of $800 per exam. Since June 2020, OVS has
reimbursed providers based on specific services rendered during the exam, medications dispensed,
age of the patient, and whether a sexual offense evidence collection kit (SOECK) was obtained during
the exam.
For FRE claims received on or before February 2, 2026, OVS is statutorily obligated to reimburse
providers at the New York State (NYS) Medicaid rate for services. For FRE claims received on or after
February 3, 2026, OVS will reimburse providers at usual and customary rates for services rendered to
perform an FRE. OVS will reimburse providers at the NYS Medicaid Fee-for-Service (FFS) rates for
pharmaceuticals dispensed as part of, and at the time of, the exam.5
State government agencies are not permitted to tell providers how to code medical records for billing
purposes. The information presented in this manual is intended to give consistent billing guidance to
medical providers to aid in completing FRE claims and billing forms. These reimbursement policies are
not intended to dictate medical or prescribing practices. Detailed guidance on provider credentialing,
training, and healthcare services related to FREs is issued by the NYS Department of Health.

FRE Claim Form
Providers must submit a signed copy of the FRE Direct Reimbursement Claim Form for each FRE
performed. Copies of this form are included in each SOEC kit, and the form can also be found on the
OVS website: https://ovs.ny.gov/FRE. Distribution of SOEC kits is managed by the NYS Department of
Criminal Justice Services (DCJS). DCJS provides NYS SOECKs at no cost to hospitals and other
health care providers. More information can be found on the DCJS website:
https://www.criminaljustice.ny.gov/evidencekit.htm.
Submission of the FRE Direct Reimbursement Claim Form is mandatory to receive reimbursement from
OVS. This form captures the information required to process a claim. All sections must be completed to
be eligible for reimbursement. To view an annotated copy of this form, see Appendix A at the end of
this document. You can download a copy of this form at the following link: https://ovs.ny.gov/fre-claimapplication-medical-providers.
OVS cannot provide guidance or training on how to code medical records or bill for services rendered
or pharmaceuticals dispensed. If providers require technical assistance, OVS recommends seeking
training from reputable online resources and/or working with a trained and certified professional medical
coder to maximize reimbursement. More information and support can also be found on the International
Association of Forensic Nurses website: https://www.safeta.org/page/paymentfactsheet/. Providers
seeking guidance on coding HIV-related diagnoses and services can find more information from the
National Alliance of State and Territorial AIDS Directors in this 2023 Billing and Coding Guide:
https://nastad.org/sites/default/files/2023-10/PDF-HIV-Prevention-BillingAndCoding-101223.pdf.

g. Survivor Billing Choices
Survivors have the option to have OVS reimburse the FRE provider directly for the cost of services,
have the FRE provider submit a claim on their behalf to their health insurance, or pay for the services
out of pocket. Selecting more than one method of payment is not allowable. If the survivor chooses to
have the provider seek reimbursement from OVS, the provider may not submit a claim for balance
billing to the survivor’s health insurance or directly to the survivor (out of pocket), per NYS Public Health
Law and OVS Regulations.

3. Procedure Codes
The billing forms may include any valid Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) or Healthcare
Common Procedure Coding System (HCPCS) codes for services rendered in the collection of
specimens or any other services the provider deemed medically necessary as part of the FRE. If
utilizing the OVS Billing Form, the provider must list all services rendered as part of the FRE, using
the free-text fields to enter any billing codes not pre-filled and additional sheets as necessary. The
services listed on the billing form must match the medical records, and OVS has the authority to
request medical records associated with any FRE. Failure to provide copies of requested medical
records may result in delayed or denied reimbursement.
4. Pharmaceuticals
OVS reimburses providers for the cost of pharmaceuticals dispensed as part of an FRE, including
but not limited to medications for HIV PEP treatment. Charges for pharmaceuticals dispensed as
part of, and at the time of, the FRE should be itemized on the FRE Pharmacy Addendum. A copy of
this form can be found on the OVS website: https://ovs.ny.gov/fre-billing-form.
Providers must use the FRE Pharmacy Addendum or standardized billing form, like UB-04, to claim
reimbursement for pharmaceuticals dispensed as part of the forensic rape exam, including but not
limited to HIV PEP medications. Pharmaceutical reimbursement is based on the NYS Medicaid
Formulary File (Formulary File) fee schedule. Only codes listed on this fee schedule will be
reimbursed. However, OVS will maintain a fee schedule history and reimburse for pharmaceuticals
previously listed in the Fee Schedule, that were valid on the date of service but may no longer be
valid at the time of claim submission or processing, at the most recent Maximum Reimbursable
Amount (MRA) rate available. More information about this file can be found on the NYS Department
of Health eMedNY website at https://www.emedny.org/ProviderManuals/Pharmacy/. Questions
about MRA rates in the Formulary File should be directed to NYS Department of Health, Office of
Health Insurance Programs.
Institutions may submit an FRE Pharmacy Addendum for pharmaceuticals that were prescribed and
dispensed as part of, and at the time of, the exam. Providers seeking reimbursement for
prescriptions, including HIV PEP, prescribed as part of, and at the time of, the FRE and filled at the
hospital pharmacy associated with the hospital submitting the FRE claim, may submit the FRE
Pharmacy Addendum and subsequent invoices to OVS for pharmaceutical reimbursement.
In the event a victim/survivor declines to receive HIV PEP medication from the FRE billing provider,
victims/survivors or their guardians may submit an OVS victim compensation claim application to
request reimbursement for out-of-pocket costs associated with HIV PEP. Please note that victim
compensation claims are subject to the agency’s payer of last resort law which means the
victim/survivor’s health insurance must be billed prior to receiving reimbursement from OVS. If the
victim/survivor does not have health insurance, or HIV PEP medications are not a covered benefit,
OVS may reimburse the victim/survivor or the guardian’s out-of-pocket costs. For more information
on the victim compensation program, please visit https://ovs.ny.gov/victim-compensation.

III.

Reimbursement
a. Eligible Services
OVS reimburses medical providers for costs associated with performing sexual assault medical forensic
exams, with or without procedures necessary for a SOECK or dispensing medication, including HIV
PEP medications.
OVS reimburses providers for services rendered to conduct an FRE, including costs associated with
gathering forensic evidence as part of a SOECK. There are currently no standardized medical billing
codes for SOECK collection procedures, nor evaluation and management specifically for SOECK
collection. Providers submitting reimbursement claims to OVS are expected to submit an OVS Billing
Form for specific services and procedures associated with conducting the FRE, including any
procedures necessary for a SOECK. Multiple providers may not bill for the same services rendered to
the same patient.
Only services rendered as part of the FRE can be reimbursed under the FRE claim. Procedures and
other services that are deemed to not be related to the FRE may not be reimbursed as part of the FRE
claim. This will be reflected in any explanation of benefits documents provided by OVS. If providers
have additional questions about why certain charges were not reimbursed, they may contact OVS at
FRE@ovs.ny.gov and request more information, including a meeting to discuss specific claims.
Follow-up visits are not covered under the FRE claim. Medical providers are encouraged to refer
patients to victim assistance programs to submit claims to the victim compensation program to cover
any out-of-pocket costs associated with follow-up care following a sexual assault that are not part of the
FRE. Please note: OVS is the payer of last resort. That means, if a patient has health insurance, and
they are seeking reimbursement for crime-related expenses outside the scope of the FRE, their
insurance must be billed first. Any expenses or amounts not covered by insurance can be submitted to
OVS for reimbursement. More specifically, patients must first bill their health insurance for out-of-pocket
costs that may be related to the original sexual assault but not rendered at the time of the FRE.

c. Reimbursement Rates
Accepting reimbursement from OVS for costs associated with performing an FRE and dispensing
medication acknowledges that the billing provider accepts this reimbursement as payment in full. The
billing provider may not bill victims/survivors or their guardians for the difference between charged
amounts and the amount reimbursed by OVS.
OVS reimburses for services rendered based on standard medical billing codes. For FRE services,
billing providers should utilize valid, standard billing codes, that is, CPT and HCPCS codes, to denote
services rendered on the billing form. Billing providers should utilize National Drug Codes (NDCs) to
classify which pharmaceuticals were dispensed as part of, and at the time of, the FRE.
Effective February 3, 2026, reimbursement rates for services rendered to conduct the FRE are based
on usual and customary amounts reimbursed by insurance companies to medical providers in NYS.
OVS worked with FAIRHealth to develop a fee schedule. Some of the services commonly registered as
part of an FRE do not have a usual and customary allowed amount under FAIRHealth’s methodology.
In these instances, OVS will determine the allowed amount. The procedure code fee schedule is posted
publicly on the OVS website, and rates will be updated at least annually.
Reimbursement rates for pharmaceuticals are based on the NYS Medicaid pharmaceutical fee
schedule, and valid NDCs for reimbursable drugs can be found at the link for pharmacy fees in the
Pharmaceuticals section. These codes may be recorded on the FRE Pharmacy Addendum.
Pharmaceutical reimbursement is based on the allowed amount or MRA cost at the time the claim is
processed by OVS.
OVS will only pay providers the per unit allowed amount based on the fee schedule and Formulary File,
regardless of how much was charged to OVS for that service or pharmaceuticals. OVS will pay the sum
of the allowed amounts on a given claim, per the fee schedule and Formulary File, up to the statutory
caps. Providers will be reimbursed for the total allowed amounts associated with services rendered, lab
services, and pharmaceuticals dispensed as part of, and at the time of, the exam up to the tier caps. It
is the responsibility of the billing provider to ensure the standard billing forms itemize reimbursable
services with appropriate and acceptable procedure codes and NDCs.
Reimbursement is bill-type specific. For services rendered in hospitals or other institutional settings,
both physician/professional and institutional bills can be submitted for the same exam. Providers will be
asked to provide their National Provider Identifier (NPI) numbers on bills.

d. Reimbursement Policies
1. Tiered reimbursement maximums will be applied to the sum of all bills submitted as part of the
same claim.
a. Providers submitting both institutional and professional bills, along with an FRE Pharmacy
Addendum, are encouraged to submit all billing forms/invoices at the same time.
b. Bills are reimbursed up to the claim tier maximum allowed amount across all bill types.
c. OVS is not responsible for dispersing equally between separate bills/invoices if providers
choose to submit institutional and professional bills. It is the responsibility of the billing providers
and physicians to determine how to disperse reimbursement between different cost centers
within their institution.
d. Providers who submit bills for additional pharmaceutical charges at a later date will still be
subject to reimbursement under the overall claim cap.
e. Additional bills submitted for the same victim, date of crime, date(s) of service, bill type, and
provider may be rejected as duplicate claims.
2. If the amount charged is less than the allowed amount for a given billing code, the amount charged
will be reimbursed. If the amount charged is greater than the allowed amount for a given billing
code, the allowed amount will be reimbursed.
3. If procedure code modifiers are present, reimbursement will be based on the amount for that given
modifier provided a different rate is reflected in the fee schedule.
4. Only services and pharmaceuticals that are related to the forensic rape exam may be reimbursed
under an FRE claim. Other charges billed on the claim but found to be unrelated to the exam will
not be reimbursed under an FRE claim.
a. For example, if a patient presented in the ER for a sexual assault, but they were also found to
have uncontrolled diabetes, services and medication related to the patient’s diabetes would not
be reimbursed by OVS under an FRE claim.
5. OVS-Specific Codes: Periodically, OVS may create agency-specific codes for services and
determine the usual and customary allowed amount for each service. These services, and their
allowed amounts, will be posted on the OVS website. Line-level reimbursement for these services
or pharmaceuticals will be subject to the overall claim maximums like other standard billing codes.
6. Reimbursement Methodology
a. Procedure Claim Line-Level Reimbursement = Units per code * Allowed Amount
i. If charged amount is ≥ allowed amount, then allowed amount
ii. If charged amount is < allowed amount, then charged amount
b. Pharmaceutical Claim Line-Level Reimbursement = Units per code * MRA Cost
i. Reimbursement is based on the MRA cost at the time the claim is processed by OVS.
ii. If charged amount is ≥ MRA cost, then MRA cost
iii. If charged amount < MRA cost, then charged amount
iv. If units ≥ maximum quantity, then OVS will reimburse up to the maximum quantity
v. If units < maximum quantity, then OVS will reimburse for the units ordered
c. Claim Header Reimbursement Amount = Sum of all procedure and pharmaceutical line-level
reimbursements

IV.

Claim Rejections and Resubmissions
FRE claims that are rejected will be returned to providers along with the reason(s) for rejection.
Providers may resubmit corrected claims containing the requisite information, and OVS will reprocess
the claim. Resubmitted claims may be sent to OVS beyond the 180 days from the date of service listed
on the claim provided the original claim was submitted within the 180 days from the date of service. The
following is a list of common rejection reasons that providers often see for FRE claims. Please note,
this is not an exhaustive list.
Sample List of Common FRE Claim Rejections
The date of service indicated on the attached invoice does not match the date of exam listed
on the FRE claim form.
The FRE claim form was not received within 180 days of the date of the exam.
The FRE claim form indicated that the facility directly provided HIV PEP meds to the patient but
did not indicate the costs associated with the HIV PEP meds on the invoice. If HIV PEP meds
were provided, but no reimbursement is being requested for the costs associated with the
medications, supporting documentation indicating that the medication was provided to the
patient must be submitted. If HIV PEP meds were only prescribed to the patient, the FRE
claim form should indicate that the medications were not provided.
A valid sexual assault diagnosis code was not indicated on the invoice.
The date of birth given on the FRE claim form is different from the date listed on the attached
invoice. Please confirm the victim’s date of birth.
The application is not fully completed: The date of crime is incomplete or missing. If an exact
date of crime cannot be determined, please provide the month and year or season and year.
The application is not fully completed: The victim/legal guardian must select and initial one of
the options provided in Section Three. Please note that, beginning with all claims received for
the date of service 2/1/21 and continuing thereafter, we will be returning every claim that does
not include the victim/legal guardian’s initials.
The total invoice amount is not equal to the sum of the treatment amounts.
The application is not fully completed: The location of crime (city) is missing or incomplete.
The victim's date of birth given on the FRE claim form is different from the victim's date of birth
listed on the OVS Billing Form. Please confirm the victim's date of birth.
At least one valid physical examination billable code was not submitted for all victims.
The required documents were not included with the application, specifically: Itemized bill with
proper billable codes.
