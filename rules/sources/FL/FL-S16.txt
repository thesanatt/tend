Bureau of Victim Compensation Schedule of Benefits
Effective January 6, 2022

{Florida Administrative Codes 2A-2.2003, 2A-2.018, and 2A-3.002}
The Bureau of Victim Compensation may provide financial assistance for eligible persons, but only after
all other sources of payment have been exhausted. Payments accepted by in-state providers on behalf of
victims are considered payment-in-full pursuant to s. 960.14 (4), Fla. Stat. The current benefit rate for
payments to treatment providers is 50% or less depending on the availability of funds. All awards made
are in accordance with the discretion and direction of the department. Benefits may be reduced or denied
without prior notice or appeal rights.

Maximum Benefit Amount
(up to)

Total benefits paid on a single claim cannot exceed $15,000 when the victim is not deceased, or catastrophically injured, $25,000 when the victim is deceased, $50,000 when the victim has sustained a
catastrophic injury as defined in s. 960.03(1), Fla. Stat., as a direct result of the crime, or $50,000 when an emergency responder was killed while answering a call for service in the line of duty. Benefits
paid to medical, mental health, or grief counseling treatment providers shall be paid at a rate of 50% or less depending on the availability of funds. Property loss cannot exceed $500 on any one claim,
and a lifetime maximum of $1,000 on all claims. Benefits paid for relocation claims cannot exceed $1,500 on any one claim, and a lifetime maximum of $3,000 for the same relocation claim type.

The Office of the Attorney General, Bureau of Victim Compensation is an equal opportunity provider and employer.
